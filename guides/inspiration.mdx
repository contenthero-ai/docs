Inspiration is the research layer. It reads the social accounts you track and their posts, ranked by outlier score. It is the grounding the [pipeline](/guides/pipeline) draws on before any draft.

## Two kinds of tracked account, one list

A tracked account is either **inspiration** (a creator or competitor you watch) or **brand** (one of your own profiles). Both live in `list_tracked_accounts`, and every row reports its own `accountType`, so omit the filter to see both and pass one to narrow.

They mean opposite things, which is why the distinction is worth keeping straight: inspiration accounts tell you what works for other people, brand accounts tell you what works for you.

## Operations

| What | MCP tool |
| --- | --- |
| Tracked accounts, either kind | `list_tracked_accounts` (filter with `accountType`) |
| One account's performance | `get_tracked_account` |
| Posts, ranked by outlier score | `list_content` |
| One post in full: transcript, analysis, scenes, its videos and their cut lists | `get_content` (`cuts: true` for the cut lists) |
| A post's Break It Down, or its scenes | `analyze_content` (`kind: "breakdown"` or `"scenes"`) |
| A post's videos (its one video, or each video slide of a carousel), downloaded into shared analysis copies, each with its cut list | `analyze_content` (`kind: "video"`) |
| A downloaded video seen and heard: frames at any seconds, its sound measured | `view` (`contentId`, and `slide` for a carousel) |
| A downloaded video's words, word by word | `transcribe` (`contentId`, and `slide` for a carousel) |

Reads need no scope; moving a tracked account within its list needs `inspiration:write`. `analyze_content` needs `studio:generate`. With `kind: "video"` it downloads every video the post holds, its one video or each video slide of a carousel, into shared analysis copies, which the reading tools use; nothing lands in your library or counts against your storage. Once stored, each video answers with its cut list (where it changes scene), free, reported by slide for a carousel. A post with no video says so: its images are already stored and readable. It is charged for what the downloads moved, and is free when those copies already exist or are being made; pass `getCost` to see the spend first. While a copy is still downloading, call `analyze_content` again with the same `contentId` and `kind: "video"` to follow it, at no charge. `get_content` reports each video's state every time (`absent` until it is downloaded), and `cuts: true` adds each downloaded video's cut list, free, narrowed by `startMs`/`endMs`. Once a video is downloaded, `view` with its `contentId` (and `slide`, the 0-based slide a carousel video is reported by) sees and hears it as a source: window it with `fromSec` and `toSec`, take frames at its cut list's seconds with `atSec` or across the window with `count` or `perSecond`, and set `sound` to measure the window as it is (loudness, where its sounds start, and a waveform and spectrogram picture). The answer names the post, never its copy's address. `transcribe` with its `contentId` (and `slide`) transcribes a downloaded video word by word: the post's one video becomes its transcript, which `get_content` then returns word-timed with `transcript: "segments"`, and a carousel slide's words stay with that slide. It spends credits, charged once to whoever asks first for what was heard, and is free once the words exist or are being made; pass `getCost` to see the spend first. A long video answers running: call `transcribe` again with the same post to follow it, at no charge. A post's video is not an import: `import` always brings something into your own library or a new project. Full parameters are in the [inspiration tool reference](/mcp/tools/inspiration).

## What an outlier score is

An outlier score measures how far a post overperformed **its own creator's baseline**, not the platform's. A small account's breakout ranks above a large account's routine post, which is what makes the list useful for finding transferable patterns rather than just popular accounts.

`list_content` spans both the creators you watch and your own posts by default. Every row carries `isOwn`, so one call answers both "what is working for the people I watch" and "how did my own posts do". Narrow with `scope` when you want only one.

## Scoping to a brand kit

`list_tracked_accounts` and `list_content` accept a `brandKitId` so you see only what is linked to one brand. If a scoped read comes back empty, the brand kit may have no linked accounts; check `get_brand_kit` for its `inspirationAccounts` and `brandAccounts`.

## Mining outliers (the two-step)

Outliers carry the signal that makes a draft perform. List them, then go deep on the best.

<Steps>
  <Step title="List">
    `list_content` (scoped with `brandKitId`) to find the top performers, already ranked by outlier score.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Get content">
    `get_content` on the top few for the full detail: hashtags, keywords, audio, and the structure you want to learn from. The transcript is **opt-in**, because a long video is a large document: pass `transcript: "text"` for the whole thing or `"segments"` for timed slices, then narrow with `startMs`, `endMs` or `transcriptSearch`. To read a video scene by scene, run `analyze_content` with `kind: "scenes"` once, then pass `scenes: "map"` to `get_content`. Each scene comes with its time range, what happens and what is said; narrow with `startMs`/`endMs`. Scenes are descriptions only: to see a scene, call `view` with the post's `contentId` and an `atSec` at the scene's start or anywhere in its range. Scenes cost credits per minute of video the first time and are free to read after that.
  </Step>
</Steps>

This two-step is the Ground phase of the pipeline. The patterns you extract here are what the host LLM emulates, in the user's own voice, when it drafts.
