Your ContentHero account (balance, spend cap, plan), the platforms available to publish to, what the user is looking at, and your own work seen or heard. 4 tools.

Every tool below is exposed by the hosted MCP server at `https://mcp.contenthero.ai` and by the `@contenthero/mcp` npm package. A <Badge>write</Badge> tool changes your account; a <Badge>read</Badge> tool does not.

### `get_account`

<Badge>read</Badge>

Get your ContentHero account: the credit balance, what is available to spend, credits reserved for work in progress, what was spent this month, the monthly spend cap, the plan and auto top-up. For a tracked social account, use get_tracked_account.

Takes no parameters.

### `update_account`

<Badge>write</Badge>

Update your ContentHero account. Only the fields you pass change. spendCap sets the monthly spend cap in credits, or null removes it: every credit spent this month counts toward it, and anything that would cross it is refused until the cap resets on the 1st of the month (UTC). Changing it needs the billing:write scope and the account's owner. Returns the account as it now is.

<ParamField body name="spendCap" type="integer or null">
The monthly spend cap in credits, or null for no cap.
</ParamField>

### `view`

<Badge>read</Badge>

See what the user is looking at, and see or hear your own work or a source. A view returns only what it names: your render, a source, or the user's screen; add live to a render to also get what the user is looking at. With nothing to render and no source, it returns the live context of the open app: the active surface, the focused element, the playhead and the selection, from the most recently active session, with the live participants. capture adds a picture of the user's screen as shown now, including unsaved and transient state; it takes a moment and briefly interrupts the page. render shows your work as it will play, rendered from the saved project: one composed frame or slide, the one in focus unless frame, slideId or slideIndex names another; for a timeline, frames, a count over a range, or perSecond over a range name several. count spreads that many frames from the first frame of the window to its last; perSecond steps from the first; frames and atSec are exact. Several frames come back one by one when they fit one result and otherwise as contact sheets with each frame labeled under its tile, in pages; nothing caps how many. width is the width of each image returned, the crop's when a region is named; without width a region comes back at native pixels. Each image names the frames it shows, sampled says how many were rendered, and anything the server changed is listed in adjusted with why. trackIds and clipIds render only those tracks and clips, picture and sound, on the timeline's own frame numbers, leaving the project untouched, so the person can keep editing while you review. sound renders a range of the mix as an export mixes it, before an export's delivery leveling, and measures it (integrated loudness, true peak, loudness range, sample peak, where sounds start, stereo width, band balance), with a waveform and spectrogram picture and a link to the audio. Its sound is also analyzed and rendered as one evidence sheet, as a source's window is below, on the sound's own seconds from the start of the range; the measurement stays the loudness to deliver by. video watches a range play with its sound; where video cannot be received, as here, it returns frames across the range and the sound measured. To see and hear a source rather than your edit, name a clip of the user's with assetId (the id get_project reports in a clip's source) or mediaUrl, or a tracked post's video with contentId (and slide for a carousel) once analyze_content kind video has downloaded it, and window it with fromSec and toSec in its own seconds. A source's frames follow the same rules as your edit's, labeled with their seconds. A source read carries the source alone, and only the parts asked for. sound measures the window as it is, beside any frames; with video you watch it play. With sound, a stored source's window also carries its sound analysis for that window: speech, sound candidates with their measured descriptors, levels against the voice, offset from the beat and repetition pattern, music and beats, loudness, and each stem's level; made once per file, and made the first time its sound is read. The window also comes as one evidence sheet: its frames, the spoken words and a spectrogram strip per stem on one time axis, each sound candidate marked by its number. A candidate is measured, never named: judge what it is from its strip and its descriptors. find, where and picture ask a source, or a project's window over fromFrame and toFrame, of its picture analysis and answer in numbers rather than images, so a view with only them returns no frames. A file's picture analysis is made once and shared: a coarse one, one picture per shot, exists from upload, and the full one is made the first time the file is studied; until the full one is ready, the answer says it is coarse and how the full one stands. Every list in their answer comes a page at a time: limit sets how many of each list a page holds, and passing the answer's nextCursor back as cursor reads the next page of all of them. Anything not ready yet (a render's page, a sound analysis, an evidence sheet, a where) says when it started, when it is expected, the exact inputs that read it again (readWith, or the same inputs again when it names none), and why it has not ended when it is blocked or late. Read it again with exactly those inputs: asking for the same render again starts nothing new, since an identical render is the one already made. When the user is charged credits for what a view made, the answer says how many. Renders are ephemeral and need no live tab, only a projectId when no one is viewing. Check your work here, never with export, which makes a file the user keeps. Requires the context:read scope.

### `get_schema`

<Badge>read</Badge>

Get a vocabulary another call accepts, by kind. 'platform': with no platform, the platforms this account can publish to, each with its formats and whether a connected account exists; with a platform (and optionally a format), the fields, options (enums) and character limits a post requires per format. Ground a post's platformSettings against it instead of guessing the fields. 'timeline': the EDITOR timeline schema, in two steps. Without a name, its index: every update_timeline op with its shape, every clip and track type, and the rules every op follows. With a name, that one entry in full: an op with its rules, or a clip type with its fields (each with who writes it, its range and its allowed values), the shared field groups it has and a copy-pasteable `example` clip; the name 'animations' reads the presets add_animation, add_transition and apply_combo take. detail 'full' reads the whole schema at once. Read the index, then the entries you need, before building any clip with update_timeline. 'layer': the CANVAS schema, in the same two steps. Without a name, its index: every update_canvas op with its shape, every layer type, and the rules every op follows. With a name, that one entry in full: an op with its rules, or a layer type with its fields and the shared field groups it has. detail 'full' reads the whole schema at once. Read the index, then the entries you need, before building any layer with update_canvas. 'export': the export formats and their options per project type, so you know what export accepts. 'link': the grammar of app addresses and every noun and section it takes (what each opens, its tabs, what a tab's item names) plus the app's origin, so you can build a link to any item from its noun and id. 'code': the authoring guide for a code clip or code layer (a video, audio or image whose content is code): what the sandbox can import and what it cannot use, the box the code draws in, the sound it can synthesize, the brand prop names, the size limit, the workflow and examples; read it before writing or changing a clip's code. 'effect': every effect code or a video or image clip can use, by group, with what it does, where code imports it, and whether a clip can carry it; with a name, that effect's parameters with their ranges and defaults, and which of them keyframe on a clip. Read it before giving anything effects. 'timeline', 'layer', 'export', 'code' and 'effect' require the editor:read scope.
